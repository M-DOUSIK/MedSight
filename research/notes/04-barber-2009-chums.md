# [4] Barber et al. 2009 (CHUMS) - medication errors in care homes

**Claim it supports:** medication administration in care homes fails often, and
it fails for reasons of workload and process rather than carelessness.

**The numbers:** 256 residents across 55 UK homes, taking a mean of **8.0
medicines** each. **69.5% had one or more medication errors.** Mean **1.9
errors per resident**. Mean potential harm on a 0-10 scale (0 = no harm,
10 = death): prescribing 2.6, monitoring 3.7, administration 2.1,
dispensing 2.0.

**The causes**, which are as useful as the rates because they describe a work
environment rather than a personal failing: doctors not accessible and lacking
information when prescribing; home staff's high workload, lack of medicines
training, and **drug round interruptions**; lack of team work between home,
practice and pharmacy; inaccurate medicine records; reliance on verbal
communication; and medication administration systems that are difficult to
fill and to check.

**The caveat:** UK, 2009, and it pre-dates most of the electronic medication
administration record systems now common in care homes. It describes the
problem MedSight addresses. It does not describe today's baseline in a facility
that has since digitised.
