# [5] Chen et al. 2023 - mild cognitive impairment in nursing homes

**Claim it supports:** a substantial minority of residents have measurable
cognitive impairment, so the target group is real and describable in the
literature's own terms.

**The number:** pooled MCI prevalence **21.2%** (95% CI 18.7-23.6%), from 53
studies, 376,039 participants, 17 countries.

**The caveat, and it is large:** the screening instrument used was
*significantly associated* with the prevalence found - studies using the
Montreal Cognitive Assessment reported **49.8%**. A figure that more than
doubles depending on how it was measured must always be quoted with its method
attached.

**What this source does NOT support**, recorded because it would be easy to
overreach: this is **mild cognitive impairment**, the stage between normal
ageing and dementia. It is not a dementia prevalence figure. No traceable
pooled estimate of dementia prevalence in residential care was found and
read, so **no dementia prevalence number is claimed anywhere.** Say that the figure was not established rather than reaching for
one that was not read.

**Terminology note.** The project owner's framing was "forgetful / amnesia /
otherwise non-adherent patients". The literature's terms are **mild cognitive
impairment**, **dementia**, and for the behaviour itself **unintentional
non-adherence** (forgetting) as distinct from **intentional non-adherence**
(deciding not to take it). Those are the terms used here.
